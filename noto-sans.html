Noto Sans: A Typeface for the World

A 32-page editorial study that moves from the idea behind Noto to the details of its letterforms. Research, alphabet specimens and annotated diagrams turn the typeface itself into the subject of the book.

Designer & editor
Gargi Gaikwad
Context
MIT Institute of Design, Pune

01 / Introduction

Beginning with a question.

Why was Noto created, who designed it, and what makes it different? The opening chapter uses these questions to introduce the project and the missing-character boxes known as “tofu”. Angled headings create a lively entry point before the longer research text.

02 / Features & specimens

A family, shown in its own letters.

The second chapter moves from descriptions to specimens. Regular and italic alphabets sit at the same scale, followed by a weight catalogue and repeated letter A studies. This sequence lets readers compare posture, stroke weight and overall texture directly.

03 / Letterform anatomy

Looking closer at the letter.

Large letter samples and fine guide lines explain cap height, stems, bars, counters, ascenders and descenders. The chapter builds from individual uppercase and lowercase forms to an annotated word spread, helping the reader move between a small detail and the rhythm of a complete word.

04 / Designer & editorial system

Giving the research a rhythm.

The final chapter places the typeface in its design context and is followed by a bibliography. Throughout the book, a restrained blue-and-white palette, recurring chapter openers and generous margins connect dense information with quieter specimen pages.
