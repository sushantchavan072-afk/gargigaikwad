Rang-e-Lotus — interpret, design & construct

A collection of embroidered leather accessories that brings the geometry of Maharashtrian dot rangoli together with Mughal lotus motifs. Four everyday objects explore how one visual language changes with scale, shape and use.

Designer
Gargi Gaikwad
Materials
Leather, embroidery thread & metal fastenings
Collection
Eyewear case, iPad case, tote & laptop bag
Concept & materials01 Eyewear case02 iPad case03 Tote bag04 Laptop bag

The idea

A meeting of motif and material.

This project explores leather as a material for cultural expression through embroidery, drawing inspiration from Maharashtrian dot rangoli and Mughal lotus motifs. It brings together the geometric rhythm and symmetry of folk art with the fluid elegance of classical ornamentation.

Coral, green and cream stitches stand out against deep brown leather. Lotus forms become central motifs, while smaller geometric repeats travel around edges and corners, connecting the four pieces as a collection.

From material to making

The material study moves from leather and hardware sourcing to thread selection, pattern marking and assembly. These photographs bring the tools, surfaces and small construction decisions into the story of the finished objects.

01 / Small leather goods

Eyewear case

A compact case gives the lotus motif a clear focal point on the curved flap. Repeating stitches frame the side panels, bringing the same rhythm around the object as it turns.

The open view reveals the snap closure and embroidered inner border. Photographs in the hand show the scale of the case and how the flap opens to receive a pair of glasses.

02 / Personal accessories

iPad case

The iPad case translates the collection into a flat, envelope-like form. A lotus anchors the flap, while narrow embroidered borders define the edges and corners without filling the entire leather surface.

The reverse carries a repeated motif, extending the composition to both sides. Inside, a patterned lining adds a second layer of colour, revealed when the snap-fastened flap is lifted.

03 / Everyday carry

Tote bag

A rounded handle and soft silhouette give the tote a different character within the collection. Small motifs sit beside the central seam and at the lower corners, allowing the leather’s broad surface to remain visible.

The mirrored embroidery near the handle draws attention to the bag’s centre. Views on the shoulder and in the hand show how the shape relates to the body.

04 / Work essentials

Laptop bag

The laptop bag uses a more structured silhouette with long handles and a curved seam across the front. Small corner motifs follow the shape of the panel, while a larger four-part lotus composition occupies the side.

This shift in scale gives the bag two readings: a restrained front view and a more decorative profile. Close photographs show how the embroidery sits alongside the construction seams.

One collection, four expressions

Across the four objects, the lotus, geometric border and three-colour thread palette form a shared identity. Each piece adapts that identity to its own proportions, from an intimate flap detail to an embroidered side panel.
